Migrate from Algebraic Entry with Check Discipline

Replace familiar equals-key habits gradually while preserving estimation, units, and independent checks.

X: READY   Y: CHECK   Z: UNITS   T: SOURCE

Procedure 6 / six · Julian Voss

01 / Translation Practice

I practice translation practice by translating meaning rather than symbols. The algebraic expression is split into named subresults, and each subresult receives an estimated sign, magnitude, and unit. I then enter one packet at a time, pausing to state what X contains and what remains below it. This slows the first sessions but prevents the equals-key habit from becoming an unexplained ENTER habit.

The drill pairs translation practice with daily drills across five short problems each day. I alternate addition chains with noncommutative subtraction and division so easy successes do not conceal operand-order weakness. Parentheses disappear from the keystrokes only after their grouping has been preserved in the plan. Accuracy, recovery, and explanation are logged separately; speed is not scored until the other three stabilize.

Check discipline adds inverse checks before trust. I make a rough magnitude estimate, annotate units, repeat one calculation through a different decomposition, and use an inverse or substitution where possible. Re-entering the identical sequence is useful for detecting a stray press, but it is not independent verification. The alternate route should have a different failure pattern and should be understandable to a reviewer.

I regard migration around translation practice as complete when a busy week does not erase the method. A short error log records reversed operands, stale modes, duplicate stack lifts, and transcription faults without embarrassment. Patterns in that log determine the next drill. The purpose is not loyalty to an entry system; it is a calmer calculation practice that notices its own mistakes before they become reported values.

Field checklist

  • Write the expected magnitude and units before entry.
  • Record the starting mode and any stored-state assumptions.
  • Repeat a boundary case and an ordinary case.
  • Confirm the result through an independent route.
  • Leave enough notation for another reader to resume.
